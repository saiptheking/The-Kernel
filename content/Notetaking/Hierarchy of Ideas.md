Any source material has a main idea, with several different supporting ideas and details. Take this passage, for example:

> Implementing a company-wide recycling program will significantly reduce waste and benefit the environment. Firstly, it will reduce the amount of waste sent to landfills by 40%. Secondly, it provides a sustainable, cost-effective method for disposing of materials, saving the company money on disposal fees. Finally, a proper program fosters a culture of environmental responsibility among employees, boosting company morale and reputation.

To find the hierarchy of ideas in this passage, you must:

1. Read the passage
2. Understand the separation between different ideas(typically via transitions)
3. Find the main idea(typically at the beginning)
4. Establish how other ideas relate to that idea(i.e. does the idea add to the main idea, or refute it?)
5. Look at supporting details(evidence/reasoning) and ask yourself, “What supporting idea does this evidence/reasoning support?”

So, for the passage above, the hierarchy of ideas is:

- Implementing a company-wide recycling program will significantly reduce waste and benefit the environment.
	- It will reduce the amount of waste sent to landfills by 40%
	- It provides a sustainable, cost-effective method for disposing of materials, saving the company money on disposal fees
	- A proper program fosters a culture of environmental responsibility among employees, boosting company morale and reputation

This passage utilized a simple, two level hierarchy. Longer, more complex passages will feature multi-level, complex hierarchies. 

Finding the hierarchy of ideas breaks down the article and makes it simpler to understand, allowing you to visualize the relationships between these ideas. This can make a daunting 150-page article much simpler to understand. Finding the hierarchy of ideas also improves retention and understanding of the source material.
