Today I went to the Griffin Museum of Science and Industry. I enjoyed the exhibits there, especially the ones about storms and "The Blue Paradox" exhibit. 

However, one thing that really stood out to me on my whole trip was a common theme: pollution.

Whether it was pollution in the air or pollution in the ocean, I learned more about pollution today than I think I ever will. In the "Coal Mine" show, one of my favorites, the tour guide told us at the end about the immediacy of the global climate crisis and how close we are to a mass extinction. The climate crisis, as he stated, "was not a scientific debate." Nobody was debating whether it was real or not. It was an objective fact that it was. He left us saying "Maybe one of you could be the one who thinks pup a new way to get energy and saves humankind from mass extinction." 

Well, that got me thinking. 

I left VERY intrigued. I was always interested in engineering in climate change, and this seemed like the perfect intersection for me to specialize in. Something about it allured and intrigued me.

Which is why I am deciding to open up the "Climate Research" hub in "The Kernel" to further study air pollution and how to prevent it. Hopefully my research and work can be useful. 

Besides, I've always wanted to save all of humankind from mass extinction.
